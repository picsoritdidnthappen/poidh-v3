# claiming a bounty

A claim is your submission showing that you've successfully completed a bounty.

If creating a bounty is making a request, **claiming a bounty is presenting your evidence that the request has been fulfilled.**

On poidh, you don't simply tell people you completed a bounty. You **prove it**.

## before you submit a claim

Before submitting anything, carefully read the bounty.

Ask yourself:

* What outcome is the bounty trying to create?
* What exactly does it require?
* What evidence is expected?
* Is there anything that would cause a submission to be rejected?

Many unsuccessful claims aren't rejected because the work was poor—they're rejected because they didn't satisfy the bounty's stated requirements.

Take a few minutes to understand what the bounty is actually asking for.

_Note: Despite the name, poidh claims aren't literally only about "pics or it didn't happen". Many bounties require evidence that go far beyond a simple photo. Be thorough and detailed in creating a claim that covers all the bases._

_To learn more about the concept and why we chose this name, we recommend [brushing up on your internet history](https://www.theguardian.com/news/2015/feb/26/pics-or-it-didnt-happen-mantra-instagram-era-facebook-twitter)._

## complete the bounty

Every bounty is different.

Some ask you to:

* Visit a real-world location
* Create something
* Research a topic
* Publish content
* Solve a technical problem
* Help another person
* Organize an event

The important thing is to focus on the **outcome**, not just checking boxes.

The best claims don't merely satisfy the minimum requirements—they clearly demonstrate that the participant understood the purpose of the bounty.

---

## gather your evidence

Evidence is how you convince other people that the bounty was completed.

Depending on the bounty, that might include:

* Photos
* Videos
* Screenshots
* Links
* Documents
* Written explanations
* Onchain transactions
* Any combination of the above

[SCREENSHOT: Example claim with multiple pieces of evidence]

Before submitting, ask yourself:

> **If someone had never met me, would this evidence convince them that I completed the bounty?**

If the answer is uncertain, consider strengthening your submission.

## submit your claim

When you're ready, select **Submit Claim**.

[SCREENSHOT: Claim submission form]

Your claim should clearly explain:

* What you did
* How it satisfies the bounty
* Any links or supporting evidence
* Anything voters should know while reviewing it

Think of your claim as making your case.

The easier you make it for voters to understand your work, the easier it is for them to vote confidently.

---

## make verification easy

Remember that other people will evaluate your submission.

Help them.

Instead of making voters search through multiple links or guess what they're looking at, explain exactly what each piece of evidence demonstrates.

For example:

> **Photo 1:** Exterior of the café.

> **Photo 2:** Interior showing bookshelves.

> **Photo 3:** Google review with the required poidh easter egg visible on the laptop screen.

> **Link:** Google Business Profile containing the published review.

A well-organized claim is easier to verify than one that simply dumps a collection of links.

## understand how claims are evaluated

After you submit your claim, the bounty enters the voting process.

The people who funded the bounty review the submitted claims and decide whether they satisfy the bounty's requirements.

They aren't voting on whether they personally like your work.

They're answering a much narrower question:

> **Did this claim complete the bounty as it was written?**

See **Voting on Claims** to learn more about how this process works.

[DIAGRAM: Submit claim → Voting → Outcome]

## if there are multiple claims

Some bounties receive multiple submissions.

Each claim is evaluated independently.

Submitting first doesn't automatically guarantee success.

Likewise, submitting later doesn't automatically prevent you from winning.

The determining factor is whether your submission satisfies the bounty's requirements and receives enough support from the voters.

## common reasons claims are rejected

### the requirements weren't followed

Read the bounty carefully.

If it asks for three things and your submission only includes two, voters may reject it.

### the evidence is incomplete

Sometimes participants complete the work but don't provide enough evidence for voters to verify it.

Remember:

> **People can only vote on the evidence they can see.**

### the connection isn't obvious

Don't assume voters know what you're thinking.

Explain how your evidence relates to the bounty.

### the submission is difficult to review

If voters have to search through multiple websites, videos, or screenshots to understand what happened, they're more likely to miss important details.

Help them by organizing everything clearly.

---

## tips for successful claims

### think like a voter

Imagine you're reviewing your own claim for the first time.

Would you immediately understand what happened?

Would you believe the evidence?

If not, improve the submission before posting it.

### make your evidence tell a story

The strongest claims are easy to follow.

Someone should be able to look through your submission and understand:

1. What the bounty asked for.
2. What you did.
3. Why the evidence proves it.

### don't rely on assumptions

If something is important, show it.

If something needs explanation, explain it.

Don't expect voters to fill in missing pieces.

### quality matters

You don't need professional photography or perfect writing.

But clear photos, organized evidence, and thoughtful explanations make your claim much easier to evaluate.

# a claim is your proof

On poidh, completing the work is only part of the process.

You also need to **demonstrate** that the work was completed.

A strong claim makes verification straightforward.

It respects the voters' time.

It connects every piece of evidence to the bounty's requirements.

And it gives the community confidence that the requested outcome really happened.

**Do the work. Show the evidence. Make your case.**
